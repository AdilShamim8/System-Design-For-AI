# Fraud & Anomaly Detection Datasets

Fraud is the domain where class imbalance, asymmetric costs, and delayed labels collide — the exact trio Chapter A.5 and Lab a3 teach you to handle. There is no perfectly clean public fraud dataset (institutions guard real fraud data carefully), so every option below has a caveat worth knowing.

### Credit Card Fraud Detection (ULB / Worldline, 2016)

**What:** 284,807 real European cardholder transactions over two days in September 2013. Features V1–V28 are PCA components (confidentiality), plus raw `Time` and `Amount`; 492 frauds.

**Size:** 284,807 rows, 31 columns (~150 MB in CSV)

**Get it:** https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mlg-ulb/creditcardfraud

**License:** Open ( Kaggle dataset by ULB / Worldline, for analysis).

**Quirks & gotchas:** 0.172% positive rate — the headline lesson. PCA features are uninterpretable (you cannot tell a merchant from a country), and 2 days is too short to study drift — know these limits and say them out loud in interviews.

**Used in:** Lab a3 — thresholds, PR curves, cost of errors.

### IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection (Vesta, Kaggle 2019)

**What:** 590,540 real e-commerce transactions from Vesta's platform, with 434 features: raw amounts, device/browser fingerprints, email domain match flags, Vesta-encoded features (V1–V339).

**Size:** ~590K rows, 434 columns (~600 MB)

**Get it:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/ieee-fraud-detection/

**License:** Kaggle competition terms; research use.

**Quirks & gotchas:** Transaction **identity** and **identity-less** tables must be joined on TransactionID (only ~25% of transactions have identity rows) — a realistic data-engineering wrinkle. Heavy NaN patterns are signal, not noise.

**Used in:** Lab a3 extension.

### PaySim (2016)

**What:** Synthetic mobile-money transactions generated by an agent-based simulator calibrated on one month of real logs from an African mobile money provider. Includes type (TRANSFER, CASH_OUT, …), amount, and isFraud.

**Size:** 6,362,620 rows (~470 MB)

**Get it:** https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ealaxi/paysim1

**License:** Open (synthetic data, CC-style on Kaggle).

**Quirks & gotchas:** Only TRANSFER and CASH_OUT types carry fraud; the rest are background noise. Being synthetic, fraud patterns are cleaner than reality — good for prototyping, not for claiming production-readiness.

**Used in:** Lab a3 alternative.

### Elliptic Data Set (2019)

**What:** ~203,769 Bitcoin transactions, 234,354 directed edges (a graph). 2% labeled illicit (dark-market, scams, stolen funds), 21% licit by Elliptic; the rest unknown. Features: 167 dimensions, in temporal waves.

**Size:** ~200K nodes, ~234K edges

**Get it:** https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ellipticco/elliptic-data-set

**License:** Research (Elliptic + Kaggle).

**Quirks & gotchas:** The graph structure is the point: fraud here is a *network* problem. Time-stamped waves make it the best public dataset for studying temporal drift in fraud models.

**Used in:** Reading for A.5 / A.7.

### Sparkov Credit Card Transactions (2020)

**What:** Synthetic-but-realistic credit card transactions for 30 fake merchants, generated by a named generator (Sparkov, by Kartik Shenoy), spanning Jan 2019–Dec 2020. Includes merchant, category, gender, city, and job fields.

**Size:** ~1.8M rows (~130 MB)

**Get it:** https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/kartik2112/fraud-detection

**License:** Open (synthetic).

**Quirks & gotchas:** Fully interpretable features (unlike ULB's PCA blobs), which makes it great for feature-engineering practice; the flip side is that fraud 'signatures' are simplistic.

**Used in:** Lab a3 alternative when you want readable features.
