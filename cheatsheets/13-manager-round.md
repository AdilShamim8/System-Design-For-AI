# 13 — The Manager & Cross-Functional Round Card

*The round that isn't technical: the hiring manager, the product partner, the "values" interviewer. Same preparation rules as the panels (out loud, 90 seconds, numbers) but different kernels — here are the ones that matter, from 09d and the STAR bank.*

## The three questions that are certainties

| Question | The kernel your answer must contain |
|---|---|
| "Walk me through a project" | 90 seconds, YOUR role stated plainly, outcome with a number, then STOP — let them pick the thread |
| "Biggest mistake" | real cost + early reporting to your manager ("I told them Tuesday, not Friday") + the systemic fix |
| "Disagreement with a manager" | their position in its best form → evidence brought → committed to the decision either way ("I built their call as well as I'd have built mine") |

## The kernels for the maybes

- **"How do you like to be managed?"** — real preference + the workaround YOU own ("written context 24h ahead; I ask for it — I don't wait").
- **"Teammate underperforming?"** — talk to them first (blocked/sick/misscoped is the usual truth) → surface to the manager EARLY as project risk, never as a person complaint → "I make the risk visible while it's cheap; you make the personnel call."
- **"Everything is urgent?"** — triage frame (reversible-first, blast radius, who's blocked) + the 30-second-escalation protocol ("I bring a one-liner with my recommendation and what would change it").
- **"Why leaving?"** — pull not push: the new problem is the subject; the old job gets one neutral sentence, zero venom (they're previewing your future 1:1s about THEM).
- **"Two years?"** — direction not title ("more blast radius, mentoring") + what you're still learning (honest, survivable).

## The manager-screen five (your questions, pick 3)

1. "What does excelling at 90 days look like versus just fine?"
2. "What's the roadmap risk you're most worried about this year?"
3. "How do decisions actually get made here — consensus or a call?"
4. "What's the last thing you changed your mind about?"
5. "What would make you say in a year that hiring me was obviously right?"

## The cross-functional (PM/design) variants

- PMs probe: scope honesty ("what would you cut from v1 and why" — have a real cut, Q-AG-12's discipline), estimates ("two weeks or a range with assumptions" — always the range), and whether you argue from user impact or technical elegance (lead with the user, land on the tech).
- Design partners probe: how you handle ambiguity ("what did you do when the spec was wrong or missing"), and whether you treat their craft as a gate or a collaboration (the "I built their decision as well as mine" sentence works here too).

## The three meta-rules

1. **Managers grade "will this person make my life easier"** — every answer demonstrates either cost or relief; structure it for relief.
2. **Negativity is the only instant fail** — past employers, past managers, past teammates: one neutral sentence, then forward.
3. **The closing five are part of the interview** — candidates who interview the manager WELL are remembered as people who manage UP well; it's the same skill.
