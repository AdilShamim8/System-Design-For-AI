# STYLING — The Writing Contract

Every layer of this course follows one contract. Read before contributing; it exists because consistency is what makes a free course feel like a product.

## Voice

- Coffee with a sharp friend, not a slide deck. Second person; active voice; no lecture tone.
- Concrete before abstract: the Friday-night spinner before "system design," Marta the librarian before "retrieval augmentation."
- Explain every term on first use in plain words; italicize it; move on. Never apologize for simplicity.

## Structure

- **One idea per paragraph** (3–5 sentences), one concept per section, sections ≥150 words or merged.
- Chapters: open with a story, end with "explain it back." Labs: open with the table (prereqs/dataset/time), end with extensions and the "what you can claim" paragraph.
- Links over duplication: if it's explained elsewhere, link it — the repo's no-duplication rule is structural, not stylistic.

## Numbers and claims

- Every number that can rot gets a date stamp ("as of late 2026"). Every incident gets its primary source. Every lab number is either *ours, from a stated run* or *yours to measure* — never folklore presented as fact.
- Arithmetic in code, not in heads (the course's own rule applies to its writing: if it's a calculation, show the script).
- "We got ≈0.75" honesty: lab results are ranges with context, because setups differ.

## Formatting

- Tables for enumerable facts (≤6 columns), prose for reasoning. No giant tables that make you scroll sideways.
- Mermaid for diagrams, inline in markdown (GitHub renders it). No ASCII-art diagrams in new content.
- Bold for the load-bearing term in a paragraph — sparingly, or it stops carrying weight.
- No "End of chapter" markers, no unrequested summaries, no filler conclusions.

## What each layer adds

- **chapters/** — Bloom's order (Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate → Create) as section structure.
- **labs/** — hostile honesty: expected numbers are ranges; solutions confess mistakes; "common mistakes" sections are real ones.
- **field-notes/** — the fixed five-part anatomy (what happened / why / lessons / 30-second version / sources), always.
- **interview-prep/** — answers carry the reasoning *pattern*, never just the conclusion; every question grounds to chapters/labs.

*Adapted in spirit from the field guide's STYLING.md pattern: a short, enforceable contract beats a long style guide nobody reads.*
