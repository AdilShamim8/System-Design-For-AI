        ## The story (the hook — full chapter opens here)

        You've seen them in every engineering blog post: boxes, arrows, cylinders, dashed lines, the occasional cloud icon. They look like art. They're actually a language. This chapter teaches you to read it — fluently — in fifteen minutes.

        ---

        ## Teaching goal

        Learner can read a system design diagram and identify services, data stores, sync vs. async flows, and failure points — before being shown any AI-specific architecture.

        ---

        ## What this chapter will cover

        - The visual grammar: boxes = services, cylinders = data stores, arrows = data flow, dashed lines = async.
- Direction matters: who's asking whom? Following the arrows reveals the request path.
- Solid vs. dashed: synchronous (wait for the answer) vs. asynchronous (fire and forget).
- Reading failure into a diagram: where are the single points of failure? What dies if what dies?
- Reading scale into a diagram: where are the load balancers? Where could horizontal scaling break?
- Practice: read three real diagrams from engineering blogs. By the end, you'll see them as text, not pictures.
