        ## The story (the hook — full chapter opens here)

        It's Tuesday morning. The same model that passed every test on Friday is suddenly returning worse answers. Nothing in the code changed. Nothing in the model changed. The world changed — and your system didn't notice. Welcome to the AI version of the Friday Night Problem, where 'it worked yesterday' is no longer a defense.

        ---

        ## Teaching goal

        Learner can name the four reasons AI systems are harder to design than normal software: non-determinism, cost-per-request, data drift, and evaluation difficulty.

        ---

        ## What this chapter will cover

        - The four ways AI differs from traditional software: non-determinism, marginal cost, data drift, evaluation difficulty.
- Non-determinism: the same input can produce different outputs. What that does to testing, debugging, and trust.
- Cost-per-request: every call costs money. The death of 'infinite free users.'
- Data drift: the world changes, the model doesn't, until you retrain. The silent killer.
- Evaluation: 'it sounds right' isn't a metric. Why AI evaluation is a discipline, not an afterthought.
- How these four shape every subsequent chapter in the curriculum.
