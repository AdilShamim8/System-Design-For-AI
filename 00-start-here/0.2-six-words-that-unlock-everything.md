        ## The story (the hook — full chapter opens here)

        Every craft has its six words. Cooking has 'salt, fat, acid, heat.' Music has 'melody, harmony, rhythm, timbre.' System design has its own six — and once you own them, every conversation about AI architecture stops sounding like a foreign language.

        ---

        ## Teaching goal

        Learner can define, in plain English with one analogy each: client/server, request/response, latency vs. throughput, scaling, and tradeoffs.

        ---

        ## What this chapter will cover

        - Client and server: the asker and the answerer. One paragraph, one analogy (the restaurant).
- Request and response: the atomic unit of all client-server communication. Why everything is built on this.
- Latency vs. throughput: the two axes of performance, often confused. Why you can optimize one and hurt the other.
- Scaling: vertical (bigger server) vs. horizontal (more servers). Why horizontal is harder but cheaper.
- Tradeoffs: the central act. Fast, cheap, reliable — pick two. Every design decision gives something up.
- A glossary pointer: these six are the floor. The rest of the curriculum builds on them.
