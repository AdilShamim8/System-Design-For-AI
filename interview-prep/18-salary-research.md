# Salary Research — How to Price Yourself Without Fooling Yourself

**Why this file exists:** "what are your expectations?" is a negotiation question wearing an HR costume, and walking in without researched numbers means answering with vibes (or worse, your last salary). This guide is the research stack and the honest-discount method. No specific numbers here — they rot; the sources and the method don't.

## The four-source triangulation

| Source | What it's good for | Distortion to correct for |
|---|---|---|
| levels.fyi | Self-reported big-tech + startup comp, by level, with equity breakdowns | Self-selection: high offers get posted more; startup equity is hope-flavored |
| Robert Half / Hays / Michael Page salary guides | Enterprise, finance, consulting, non-bay markets; percentiles by role | Lag the market ~6–12 months; broad role buckets blur AI-specialist premiums |
| Posted-range job listings (CO/CA/NY/WA disclosure laws) | The LEGAL floor and ceiling — real bands, real companies, current | Ranges are often posted wide to game the law; the top of a posted range is usually a stretch |
| Blind / teamblind + peer DMs | Directional culture/comp reality, refresh policies, negotiation anecdotes | Anonymity rewards extremes and grievance; treat as rumors with a hit rate |

**The triangulation:** for your target role+level+market, collect the median from each source, drop the highest and lowest, average the rest. That's your anchor zone; your ask sits at the 60–75th percentile of it (leaving room to be negotiated down TO your real number, not below it).

## The honest-discount method for equity

Paper equity is not cash. The discount ladder (rule of thumb, stage-dependent):

- **Public company RSUs:** ~full value (liquid, taxed at vest) — count as cash with a vesting schedule.
- **Late-stage private (Series D+, rumored IPO):** 50–75 cents on the dollar, IF the last round wasn't a down round and the preference stack isn't ugly. Ask: "what's the preference stack and the 409A?" — the questions themselves signal sophistication.
- **Early-stage (Seed–B):** 10–30 cents. Option value = (probable exit) × (your strike-adjusted share) — most startups return zero; a few return the fund. Size the bet by what you can afford to have go to zero, not by the founder's projection.
- **The refresh question belongs in the research phase:** a company with strong refresh grants (new equity yearly for performers) can pay 20% less initial equity and still beat a bigger one-time grant over 4 years. Ask HR directly — it's a normal question, and the answer changes the math.

## The AI-specialist premium (and its honest boundary)

Demand for production-AI skills (this course's target: systems, evals, RAG/agents, cost) has outpaced supply — premiums of 10–30% over generalist SWE comp at the same level are common at AI-native companies, and title inflation runs hot ("AI Engineer" spans $120K–$500K+ packages across the market). The honest boundary: the premium attaches to DEMONSTRATED production work (your labs, your incidents, your numbers — the artifacts this course builds), not to the label. In the interview, the premium is earned by the round-2 banks' ops-fluency, not the job title on your resume.

## Level research (the comp multiplier nobody prices)

A level miss costs 15–40% — more than any negotiation. Before ANY loop: search the company's leveling on levels.fyi/Blind ("L4 vs L5 scope at X"), and ask the recruiter verbatim: "What does the leveling rubric say distinguishes this level from the next one up?" Then interview AT the target level's bar: the calibration guide's staff/senior tells are worth real money.

## The expectations answer (the script)

When they finally ask, with research done:

> "Based on my research for this level and market — levels.fyi, the posted bands, and where my production-AI experience sits — I'm targeting [X–Y] total comp, with flexibility on the structure. If the level and scope are right, the numbers will work."

Three properties: a researched range (not a point), structure-flexibility (opens non-cash levers), and scope-conditionality (signals you're leveling-conscious). If they push for your current comp: "I'd rather anchor on the market for this role — I'm targeting [range]" — in most jurisdictions they can't require the old number.
