# 21 — The First 90 Days (After You're Hired)

*The selection round's hidden sequel: performance in the first 90 days determines the reputation that compounds for years — and interviewing skills (structured thinking, measured claims, scoped promises) are exactly the skills that make it go well. This is the playbook for converting the offer into trust.*

## Days 1-30: measure, don't build

- **The listening tour (scheduled week 1):** your manager (what does success look like at 90 days? what broke last year?), each teammate (what should I NOT touch? what do you wish someone would fix?), the adjacent teams (what do they think your team does badly?).
- **The instrumentation pass:** read the incident history and the dashboards before proposing anything — are the numbers real? (BQ-9's humility applied to your new codebase: a metric that can hide a third of the traffic is a filter, not a metric.)
- **Find the eval set — or note its absence.** The presence and quality of the eval harness tells you more about the team's maturity than any roadmap doc. Absence is your first 90-day opportunity, not a criticism to make.
- **Ship ONE small thing (week 2-4):** a slice cut on an existing metric, a dashboard nobody built, a flaky test fixed — earn the right to propose bigger by shipping smaller first.
- **The promises ledger:** write down everything you said in the interview process ('I'd want to look at your eval story') — those promises are now due, and the team remembers them.

## Days 31-60: one real project, scoped

- **Pick by leverage, not by glamour:** the thing that unblocks others beats the thing that impresses. The BQ-12 answer, enacted: instrument, then earn bigger proposals.
- **Import the interview discipline:** pre-registered success metrics before starting, a written rollback plan, weekly demos (the same structure that won the offer, now generating trust internally).
- **The first 'no':** you WILL be asked to build something you'd advise against (Q-AG-12's invoice-agent moment, internal edition). Deliver it the same way: alternatives with specific disqualifiers, not a refusal.

## Days 61-90: the mandate conversation

- **The 90-day writeup (2 pages max):** what I measured, what I shipped, what I now believe the team's biggest risk is, what I propose to own next — this document IS your real interview for the next two years.
- **The stakeholder map is now yours:** you know who decides, who unblocks, who blocks. The org-reading skills from the calibration guide apply inward: same behaviors, different levels.
- **Set the next 90's ONE goal with your manager — in writing.** The candidates who become the engineers people fight to keep are the ones who made 'what does success look like' a standing question, not an interview line.
