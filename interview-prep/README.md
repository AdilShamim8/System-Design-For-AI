## The rules of this layer

1. **Out loud or it didn't happen.** Reading a mock is rehearsal-shaped procrastination. Timer, paper, voice.
2. **Write before you read.** Every bank question gets YOUR 5-minute answer sketch before the worked one. The gap between them is your study plan.
3. **Numbers over adjectives.** You have lab numbers nobody else has — your hit rates, your p99s, your cost curves. Use them; they are your moat.
4. **One framework, every question.** The 6 steps (below) are the skeleton; the content changes, the skeleton never does. Automatic skeleton = thinking capacity for content.

## How the mocks work

Each mock: a realistic prompt, then a candidate-quality walkthrough with time markers, the interviewer's follow-ups (the curveballs), and a scoring rubric. **Attempt the mock cold** (45 min, paper, recorded), then read the walkthrough, then grade yourself against the rubric, then do one more mock the same day while the embarrassment is fresh. Two mocks per day is plenty; three is noise.

## What "ready" means

From [PROGRESS.md](../PROGRESS.md): you can complete any two mocks at strong-hire quality out loud in 45 minutes; you can state your p99 story, your A/B math, your RAG failure modes, and one real incident per design principle — from memory. When that's true, you're not hoping anymore. You're scheduling.
