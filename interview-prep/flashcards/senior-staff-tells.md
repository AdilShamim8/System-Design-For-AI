# Flashcards — Senior & Staff Tells

20 cards. The sentences and moves that mark levels, from the calibration guide and the round-2 banks. Drill these until the senior ones come out of your mouth unprompted.
---

**The junior-to-mid tell.**

> 'My lab measured X, so I'd expect roughly X here' — evidence entering the vocabulary. Numbers with provenance replace adjectives.

**The mid-to-senior tell.**

> 'We're not asking whether the model is better; we're defining what evidence would make us stop asking' — process replacing assertion (Q-ML-14).

**The senior-to-staff tell.**

> 'The principle that survives model changes is grounding-first, cite-or-refuse — so that's the level we standardize, not the prompt' — decisions handed to others as principles.

**What each level opens with.**

> Junior: a component list. Mid: a requirements recap. Senior: the job to be done + what it's worth. Staff: the org/platform frame (calibration guide's table).

**The numbers behavior per level.**

> Junior: none. Mid: present when asked. Senior: volunteered, with derivations. Staff: volunteered, with confidence bounds AND what would change them.

**The alternatives behavior per level.**

> Junior: one design. Mid: two options, picks one. Senior: options with the specific disqualifier per loser. Staff: decisions as principles that settle future variants.

**Failure-mode talk per level.**

> 'We'd monitor' (junior) → names 2-3 per component (mid) → names the class + detection + blast-radius cap (senior) → failure modes as a PORTFOLIO: which ones the business can survive (staff).

**Pushback behavior per level.**

> Folds or doubles down (junior) → defends with reasons (mid) → defends with evidence, concedes with specifics (senior) → finds the third option nobody offered (staff).

**The one-gap diagnosis, stuck at junior.**

> No numbers anywhere. Fix: every claim gets a magnitude — the labs exist to fill this (calibration guide).

**The one-gap diagnosis, stuck at mid.**

> Everything asked-for, nothing volunteered. Fix: pre-empt — state the option set and disqualifiers BEFORE the interviewer asks.

**The one-gap diagnosis, stuck at senior.**

> Wins every technical exchange, loses the frame — accepts the problem as given. Fix: reframe first ('the question underneath is one bot or a platform') and price the not-building option out loud.

**The one-gap diagnosis, stuck at staff.**

> All strategy, no load-bearing — two-year principles with no Q1 critical path. Fix: anchor every principle to the first concrete deliverable and its owner.

**The two-question check for level inflation.**

> Could this candidate TEACH the junior version (senior+)? Could they defend the not-built list to a VP (staff)? Your level is the lower of the two honest answers.

**The 3 AM test for architecture seniority.**

> Every incident response element — schema validation, versioned rollback, circuit breakers, trace-first debugging — was an architecture decision made months earlier. 3 AM is when architecture pays its bill (Q-AG-10).

**The governance move that marks staff.**

> Agent scope creep is a governance problem disguised as a roadmap: the capability checklist (inventory, authority alignment, injection surface, eval coverage, blast-radius math per tool) IS the governance (Q-AG-14).

**The 'what we won't build' discipline.**

> V1 restraint, named: no multi-agent framework (market consensus shifts; premature abstraction calcifies), no fine-tuning infra (no eval set justifies it), no visual builder — say the reason per cut (Mock 12).

**The escalation move under disagreement.**

> Data up, tone flat: 'dependency signals go on the risk register with a named owner; my job is making the risk visible and owned, not winning the argument by veto' (Mock 11's curveball 3).

**The honest-number move.**

> State the confidence band with the number: 'directional, not statistically tight — 50 questions per slice; the CI matters before betting the quarter.' Senior candidates price their own uncertainty.

**The frame-refusal move.**

> 'Fine-tune vs RAG is the wrong unit — the jobs decompose: extraction is fine-tuning-shaped, Q&A is retrieval-shaped; the real architecture is probably both' (Mock 10). Rejecting a false binary is a level signal.

**The composure tell (the last one).**

> Silence after an interviewer's hard question, then structured answer — candidates who fill silence with filler lose points they earned elsewhere. Think, then answer in structure.
