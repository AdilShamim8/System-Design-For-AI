# The 72-Hour Protocol — Final Countdown to the Onsite

**What this is:** the last three days before an onsite are worth more than the three weeks before them — not for learning anything new (you won't; inputs after T-72h don't consolidate), but for state management, logistics, and rehearsal of what's already in your head. This is the hour-by-hour protocol.

## T-72h (three days out): the freeze and the forecast

- **Knowledge freeze.** No new banks, no new mocks, no new frameworks. Everything added now displaces something consolidated. The next 72 hours are for COMPOSURE, which is a performance variable you can actually train.
- **The forecast pass (45 min):** write the predicted loop — round types in order, the interviewer's likely specialty (LinkedIn the panel if names are shared), and which of YOUR artifacts maps to each round. Gaps in the map = tomorrow's rehearsal slots, not new material.
- **Logistics lock:** route to the office (or the video setup test: camera at eye level, headphones, wired internet, backup hotspot charged), outfit chosen (one notch above their daily dress code), and the day's food decided. Every micro-decision eliminated now is attention available then.
- **Sleep starts TONIGHT, not tomorrow.** Two full nights before the onsite is the minimum for reaction time and verbal fluency — the night before is too late to bank.

## T-48h (two days out): the rehearsal day

- **One full mock (90 min max)** — interviewer-run if possible (the interviewer-guide's structure). Score it with the calibration guide. This is a COMPOSURE rehearsal, not a knowledge check: you're rehearsing your recovery moves, your narration, your 60-second opens.
- **The STAR pass (30 min):** your top 5 stories, one-liners only, out loud. If a story needs refreshing it should have been retired at T-72h.
- **The numbers pass (15 min):** your lab numbers — the ones you'll volunteer. "60% to 90% refusal," "PSI 0.1/0.2," "780K per arm," "~$0.11/resolved ticket." Exact, in your mouth, not on paper.
- **Company research (45 min):** their AI surface — who's their user, what's their obvious failure mode, one intelligent question per round. The "why us" answer gets written HERE: three sentences connecting THEIR product to YOUR labs.
- **Stop by early evening.** A tired rehearsal is worse than none.

## T-24h (the day before): taper

- **One round-2 bank section, re-read passively.** Familiar material only — the goal is activation, not acquisition.
- **The pre-brief card (cheatsheet #12):** read the round-by-round table once. Pack it mentally.
- **Prepare the environment:** printed resume ×5 (onsite), water bottle, the charger, the outfit laid out. Video onsite: the room's lighting checked at the ACTUAL interview hour (light changes through the day).
- **The evening cut-off:** no prep after dinner. A walk, a meal, something genuinely unrelated. Sleep is the last study session — it's when the week consolidates.

## T-0 (the day): the blocks

| Time | Block | Content |
|---|---|---|
| T-2h | Wake + fuel | Real breakfast; caffeine at your NORMAL time (a double espresso you never drink is a tremor, not an edge) |
| T-90m | Warm-up | ONE bank section or one coding SOLUTION, read aloud. The voice needs warming like a muscle |
| T-60m | Logistics | Onsite: commute with buffer. Video: setup + the room locked |
| T-30m | The 3-3-3 | 3 deep breaths · your 3 best numbers · the 3 rounds' opening lines ("I'll clarify, then design, then failure modes") |
| T-10m | Stop | Nothing new. Water. Stand up straight — posture first, confidence follows |

**Between rounds (onsite):** the reset ritual — bathroom, water, one slow breath, next round's pre-brief row. NEVER post-mortem mid-day.

**After:** send the same-day thank-yous (one technical thread per interviewer, per cheatsheet #12), then the 48-hour rule applies to outcomes: no answer-reconstruction at midnight. The log entry waits for morning.
