# Offers & Negotiation — The Playbook

**Why this file exists:** negotiation is the highest hourly rate you will ever earn — 3 conversations across 2 weeks that set 1–4 years of comp and scope. Most candidates wing it because it feels uncomfortable. This playbook makes it procedural: the timeline, the scripts, the levers, and the lines you do not cross.

## Before the offer exists (the setup does the work)

- **Anchor in the recruiter call, correctly:** "I want to make sure we're aligned on level and scope first — if that fits, I'm confident we'll land the numbers." Defer the number once; if pressed: a range whose FLOOR you'd accept, informed by the salary-research guide. Never name your current comp (in most locales they can't require it; "I'm targeting market for this level" redirects).
- **Collect leverage all loop long:** every "we're excited" is data. Note WHO said it — the hiring manager's enthusiasm is the lever that moves HR's numbers.
- **Never stop your pipeline at "we'd like to make you an offer":** the offer call is scheduled, not final. Competing processes are the ONLY leverage that reliably moves big-tech numbers.

## The offer call (30 min — mostly listening)

Let them talk. The first number is the OPENING, not the ceiling (bands have 10–30% of spread even at big tech; more at startups). Your script:

1. Thank + enthusiasm (genuine — you WANT this to be true): "I'm excited about the team and the problem."
2. The stall: "I'd like 48 hours with the details — can you send the full package in writing?"
3. One question that isn't about cash: "How does the level map to scope — what will I own in the first year?" (Signals you weight growth; primes non-cash levers.)

**Never negotiate on this call.** The first live negotiation you do should be with the full package in front of you and your numbers ready.

## The negotiation (48h later — one call or email, prepared)

The counter structure: **enthusiasm → specific ask → reason → the door stays open.**

> "I'm genuinely excited about this. The one gap is base: given [competing process at a peer company / your research for this level in this market], I'd need X to sign. Everything else works — can we get there?"

Rules:
- **One ask at a time.** Multi-front negotiations (base AND equity AND start date) signal inflexibility; pick the gap that matters, win it, the rest follows.
- **X should be 10–20% above their number**, inside the band's plausible ceiling. Ask for 40% and you're renegotiating whether they like you.
- **Competing offers are named, never invented.** "I have a final round at [company] next week" (true) moves numbers; a fabricated one that gets checked (recruiters talk) ends the conversation.
- **Silence after their response.** The first person to fill silence with a concession loses. Count to five.

## The levers beyond base

| Lever | When it moves | How to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Sign-on bonus | Almost always (it's a one-time line item, not a comp floor) | "Can you bridge the gap with a sign-on?" — the classic base-stalemate breaker |
| Equity refresh / initial grant | Mid-size and startups | "What's the refresh policy after year 1?" — signals you stay; opens the grant conversation |
| Level | The hardest, highest-value | "What would a [level+1] offer look like here?" — sometimes the band simply contains it |
| Start date | Always (costs them nothing) | Ask at accept, not in negotiation — keeps it clean |
| Scope/role definition | Small cos, always | Get the first-year mandate in writing — "I'd like the first-90-days scope in the offer letter" |

## The lines you do not cross

- **Exploding offers:** "48 hours to decide" is pressure, not policy — name the conflict: "I have a final round Tuesday; I can decide Thursday." If they truly won't wait 3 days, that's culture data.
- **Lying about numbers** (current comp, other offers): recruiters verify more than candidates believe, and the industry is small.
- **Accepting then continuing to interview:** renege costs you the bridge AND the recruiter's next placement of you. Accept = stop.
- **Negotiating after acceptance:** reneging on your own counter. If new info appears (another offer lands post-accept), the honest move is disclosure before action — some employers will match; some will release you warmly; hiding it costs both.

## The decision (72 hours, structured)

Score the final offer against the four dimensions from the process deck: cash (12-month realized), equity (value × probability discount — paper at 25–50 cents on the dollar depending on stage), level/scope (what you'll OWN), optionality (does this role compound toward your next target?). Team over company when close: the manager and the mission of the specific team predict your next two years better than the logo. Then decide once, in writing, and stop re-litigating — post-decision rumination is the thief of onboarding energy.
