# Company Tiers — Where to Aim, and What Each Loop Tests

**Why this file exists:** "apply everywhere" is a pipeline strategy for nobody. Different company tiers run different loops, price different signals, and offer different risk/learning/comp trades. This guide maps the tiers, tells you what each loop actually tests, and gives you an aiming strategy (where to practice, where to land, where to stretch).

## The five tiers (for AI system-design roles)

| Tier | Examples (shape, not endorsement) | Loop shape | What they over-test | Comp character | Risk/learning trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frontier labs | Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind-class | Research-flavored: depth over breadth, takehomes, ML theory + systems | Novelty, research taste | Very high, equity lottery-class | Extreme selectivity; learning ceiling also extreme |
| Big tech AI | Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon + their AI product orgs | Standardized loops (design + coding + behavioral), bar-raiser calibration | Scale habits, internal-tooling fluency | High cash, liquid equity, level-capped | Stable, slower decisions, your scope depends on the team lottery |
| AI-native product cos | Scale-ups where AI IS the product (assistant, copilot, safety, infra startups) | Practicals: eval design, RAG/agent depth, "walk me through what you shipped" | Production AI scars (incidents, evals, cost) | Moderate-high cash, paper equity | Highest learning-per-year; equity is a bet on the round |
| AI-in-enterprise | Fintech, health, legal, industrials adopting AI | Domain-weighted: regulation, safety, integration reality | Judgment under constraints (compliance, budget) | Moderate, stable | Deep domain compounding; slower tech pace |
| Agencies/consultancies | AI-transformation shops, internal-innovation teams | Portfolio + client-facing rounds | Communication, speed, breadth | Moderate-low, variable | Fast reps on many problems; shallow ownership per system |

## What each loop tests that the others don't

- **Frontier labs:** the *why* behind your choices, at depth. Expect "you said distillation recovers half the quantization gap — where does that number come from?" (mock 10's supply-chain curveball is the house style).
- **Big tech:** the *shape* — do you follow a recognizable structure at scale? They optimize for low variance across thousands of interviews; the 6-step framework reads as fluency here.
- **AI-native:** the *scars* — incidents you've caused/fixed, evals you've built, costs you've cut. Your lab numbers are the currency; the round-2 banks' "operations" flavor is the prep.
- **Enterprise:** the *constraints* — residency, audit, liability (Q-CC-11/14, mock 9's HIPAA chain, mock 11's refusal list). The strongest candidates here lead with the compliance frame unprompted.
- **Agencies:** the *pitch* — can you translate AI to a skeptical exec in 5 minutes? Behavioral rounds dominate; the STAR bank's delivery rules apply doubled.

## The aiming strategy

**The practice tier:** 2–3 loops at the tier BELOW your target — cheaper reps, real stakes, calibration data (your mock scores vs. real signals). Never practice at your dream company; first impressions are non-renewable.

**The pipeline tier (bulk):** 4–6 applications at your at-par tier — this is where offers actually come from. Target teams (not companies) whose product you can name and care about.

**The stretch tier:** 2–3 applications at the tier above — you lose nothing but time, and a stretch offer resets your anchoring for every other negotiation. The stretch's REAL function: the questions you couldn't answer in the stretch loop are your next 90 days' syllabus.

**The anti-portfolio:** companies whose product you'd defend poorly in the "why us" round. Enthusiasm fakes badly; save the slots.

## Sequencing with the 30/60/90 plans

The study plans assume you're mid-preparation. This guide assumes you're ready to enter the market. The bridge: start the practice-tier applications at day 30 of your plan (interviewing IS preparation), the pipeline tier at day 45–60, stretch at day 60+. You'll enter the pipeline tier with real-loop calibration instead of mock-only confidence.
