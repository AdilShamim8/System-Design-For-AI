# Interview Calibration — What Good Looks Like at Each Level

**Why this file exists:** rubrics score; calibration *places*. When you grade yourself (or a friend) on a mock, the raw points don't answer the question you actually have: "what level am I interviewing at, and what's the gap to the next rung?" This file is the placement guide — the observable behaviors per level, the tell-tale sentences, and the one gap that usually separates a candidate from the next rung.

## The observable behaviors

| Dimension | Junior | Mid | Senior | Staff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opens with | a component list | a requirements recap | the *job to be done* + what it's worth | the org/platform frame |
| Numbers | none, or vague | present when asked | volunteered, with derivations | volunteered, with confidence bounds + what would change them |
| Alternatives | one design | 2 options, picks one | options with the specific disqualifier per loser | decisions as *principles* that settle future variants |
| Failure modes | "we'd monitor" | names 2–3, per component | names the class + the detection + the blast-radius cap | failure modes as a *portfolio* — which ones the business can survive |
| Pushback | folds or doubles down | defends with reasons | defends with evidence, concedes with specifics | finds the third option nobody offered |
| Scope | builds everything asked | builds v1 + roadmap | builds v1 + names what NOT to build | v1 as a wedge for the platform decision |

## The tell-tale sentences

- **Junior-to-mid tell:** "my lab measured X, so I'd expect roughly X here" — evidence entering the vocabulary.
- **Mid-to-senior tell:** "we're not asking whether the model is better; we're defining what evidence would make us stop asking" — process replacing assertion.
- **Senior-to-staff tell:** "the principle that survives model changes is grounding-first, cite-or-refuse — so that's the level we standardize, not the prompt" — decisions handed to other people, as principles.

## The one-gap-per-rung diagnosis

When grading, find the *single* dominant gap (there's almost always one):

- **Stuck at junior:** no numbers anywhere. Fix: every claim gets a magnitude — latency, cost, accuracy. The labs exist to fill this.
- **Stuck at mid:** everything is asked-for, nothing volunteered; alternatives appear only under challenge. Fix: pre-empt — state the option set and the disqualifiers before the interviewer asks.
- **Stuck at senior:** wins every technical exchange, loses the frame — accepts the problem as given. Fix: reframe first ("the question underneath this is whether we build one bot or the platform") and price the "not building" option out loud.
- **Stuck at staff:** everything's strategic, nothing's load-bearing — the two-year principles with no Q1 critical path. Fix: anchor every principle to the first concrete deliverable and its owner.

## Calibrating yourself honestly

Record your mock (audio, with consent). Listen once for the table's dimensions. The uncomfortable exercise: count the seconds of silence after each interviewer question — candidates who fill silence with filler lose level-points they earned elsewhere; candidates who *think* then answer structurally read a level higher. Then apply the two-question check from the interviewer's guide: could you teach the junior version? Could you defend the not-built list to a VP? Your level is the lower of the two honest answers, plus one rung of preparation — that's what this course's remaining days are for.
