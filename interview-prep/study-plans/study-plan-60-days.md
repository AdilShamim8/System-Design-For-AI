# The 60-Day Plan — Learn While Preparing

**For:** working engineers targeting a role in ~2 months. Balanced: 60% new learning (the labs you haven't done), 40% interview conversion. **10–12 hours/week.**

## Weeks 1–3 — The missing labs (the DO layer)

Pick your weak track and actually run its labs (not read — run):

- **ML-flavored:** a2 (recommender), a3 (fraud), a7 (drift) — 3 labs, ~8 hours each with journaling.
- **GenAI-flavored:** b2 (RAG from scratch), b4 (eval), b5 (cache) — the employable core per the field guide's job data.
- **Platform-flavored:** b1 (cost), x2 (cost dashboard), x3 (load test).

Journal after each (three sentences). Those journal entries become behavioral stories automatically — you're building interview material while building skill.

## Weeks 4–5 — The cross-cutting spine

- Read X.0–X.5 chapters if not yet; run x1 (monitoring).
- [01-the-framework.md](../01-the-framework.md) + [07-estimation-drills.md](../07-estimation-drills.md) drills 1–8.
- First mock (your role's), cold. Grade, wince, log gaps.
- Field notes: 6 of 12, one per breakfast.

## Weeks 6–7 — Interview conversion

- Question banks: your track's full bank + [05-cross-cutting](../05-cross-cutting-questions.md). Sketch-first discipline, always.
- Mocks 2 and 4 (the two most transferable designs), on consecutive days.
- The curveballs file; drill 8 aloud with a friend or a recording.

## Week 8 — Capstone-lite + peak

- Do **x4 (the design-doc drill)** end-to-end — the one-pager is a portfolio artifact AND the best synthesis exercise in the course.
- Remaining field notes; all flashcard decks in rotation (10 min/day throughout the whole plan — non-negotiable).
- Mocks 3 and one other; then follow cheat sheet #11 for the final 48 hours.

## The 60-day difference from 30

You have time to *run* labs, so every claim you make in interviews has your own numbers behind it. Guard that: if the schedule slips, cut reading, never cut labs — the labs are the part the flashcards and mocks can't replace.
