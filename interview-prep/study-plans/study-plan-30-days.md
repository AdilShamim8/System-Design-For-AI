# The 30-Day Interview Sprint

**For:** people with an interview on the calendar (or the courage to schedule one). Assumes the course's chapters are at least skimmed; this plan converts *reading* into *performance*. **8–10 hours/week. The daily constant: 10 minutes of flashcards and one spoken answer.**

## Week 1 — Diagnose and repair the spine

- **Day 1:** Take Mock 1 cold (45 min, paper, recorded — the recording is your baseline; it will be humbling, that's the point). Grade yourself against the rubric.
- **Days 2–3:** Read [01-the-framework.md](../01-the-framework.md) + [12-common-mistakes.md](../12-common-mistakes.md). Re-attempt Mock 1. The delta on attempt two is your first proof the method works.
- **Days 4–5:** [07-estimation-drills.md](../07-estimation-drills.md) — drills 1–6, on paper. Then cheatsheet #5 (latency numbers) until the AI ladder is reflexive.
- **Days 6–7:** Question bank matching your target role ([02](../02-ml-questions.md) or [03](../03-genai-questions.md)): 5 questions, YOUR 5-minute sketch first, then compare. Log the gaps — that's week 2's syllabus.

## Week 2 — Your weak track, hard

- **Days 8–10:** The labs you skipped hurt now. Reread (don't redo — no time) the SOLUTION.md of the 3 labs in your weak track: the numbers and the "five lessons" sections are the retrievable material. Write each lesson as one flashcard.
- **Days 11–12:** Mock 2 or Mock 4 (whichever matches your role). Cold, timed, recorded. Then the curveballs file — pick 5, answer aloud.
- **Days 13–14:** Field notes marathon: all 12, one per 15 minutes, writing the one-line lesson for each (the README's table is your answer key). Then: say three of them, cold, as if answering "tell me about AI failure modes."

## Week 3 — Breadth and speed

- **Days 15–17:** Drills 7–12 + cheatsheet #6 and #7 (cost math, capacity). Then the bridge bank ([06](../06-classical-bridge-questions.md)) — 4 questions: the classic-spine-holds answer pattern until smooth.
- **Days 18–19:** Two mocks back-to-back days (pick from 3, 5, 6, 8). The stamina of two consecutive design sessions is itself a trained skill.
- **Days 20–21:** [09-behavioral-questions.md](../09-behavioral-questions.md): write your six stories with numbers, say each in 90 seconds. Record. Listen once (yes, it's uncomfortable — do it once).

## Week 4 — Peak and taper

- **Days 22–24:** One mock per day, alternating tracks. After each: 20 minutes of gap-repair (the bank/cheatsheet/lab the rubric pointed at).
- **Day 25:** [11-seniority-ladder.md](../11-seniority-ladder.md) — read your target level's description, then re-listen to your day-22 recording. You'll hear the gap concretely; fix the two biggest tells.
- **Days 26–27:** [10-takehome-guide.md](../10-takehome-guide.md) if any company in your pipeline does take-homes; otherwise the estimation drills, redone from scratch, timed.
- **Days 28–29:** Cheat sheet #11 (last 48 hours) — follow it to the letter.
- **Day 30:** Light flashcards. Sleep. You're not cramming anymore; you're a person who has done this twenty times, doing it a twenty-first.

## The rule that makes 30 days work

**Every day produces one spoken artifact** — an answer, a drill, a story, a mock. Reading is prep; speaking is practice. If a day ends with nothing said out loud, that day didn't count.
