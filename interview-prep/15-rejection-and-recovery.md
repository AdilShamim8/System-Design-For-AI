# Rejection & Recovery — The 48-Hour Protocol and the Compounding Log

**Why this file exists:** at a ~20–30% onsite-to-offer rate, rejection is the loop's DEFAULT outcome — a candidate running 6 processes expects 4–5 rejections per offer. The candidates who get hired aren't the ones who never fail; they're the ones whose failures buy information. This file makes rejection a process, not a mood.

## The 48-hour rule

**Hours 0–48: feel it.** Rejection stings because it's social rejection wearing a performance costume — the brain processes it the same way. Fighting the feeling extends it; scheduling it ends it. Tell one person, close the laptop, run/swim/walk, and give yourself permission to be annoyed. No "what did they mean by..." emails at midnight.

**Hour 48: the debrief (30 minutes, written).** Answer five questions in the tracker:

1. Which round did it end in? (Screen rejections = volume problem; onsite rejections = depth problem; post-onsite = level calibration or team-fit — each has a different fix.)
2. What was the ONE question/thread I handled worst? (There's almost always one identifiable moment — the deep-dive you couldn't go a second level on.)
3. What did I NOT have in my preparation that would have changed it? (A number? A story? A framework? A concept?)
4. What signal did I get that contradicts the rejection? (Positive feedback, a round that went great — calibrates how much to trust the no.)
5. Which ONE artifact fixes this before the next loop? (A new STAR story, a bank answer, a lab run — not "study more.")

## The extraction: asking for feedback

One polite ask, low-expected-yield: "Any feedback you can share would genuinely help my preparation — I appreciated the process." Most companies return a template (liability policy), but ~1 in 4 recruiters leaks a real sentence ("the team wanted deeper eval experience"). That sentence is worth the ask. Then let it go — no follow-ups, no appeal. Re-application windows are 6–12 months; you're building a bridge to that date, not burning it.

## The compounding log

The tracker row per loop (date, company, tier, round reached, the 5 debrief answers, the artifact produced) is the actual asset. After 4–5 loops the pattern jumps out: "3 of 4 onsite rejections died in the deep-dive on scaling" — that's not bad luck, that's a syllabus. The candidates who convert loops into offers fastest are the ones whose tracker has an artifact column that fills.

## The re-entry rules

- **Loser's tilt is real:** 3 rejections in 2 weeks degrades the next loop's performance (interviewing on fumes reads as low energy). After a bad streak: one full rest week, one mock as INTERVIEWER (recovery through control), then re-enter at the practice tier.
- **The story reframe (for the behavioral rounds):** "Tell me about a failure" — your rejections ARE the material now. The strong shape: the specific miss, the artifact it produced, the next loop where it paid off. "I got rejected from X for shallow eval depth, built the eval harness (coding-8), and my next offer was at a higher tier" is a BETTER story than a fabricated smooth win — it demonstrates the growth loop employers actually can't train.
- **The 6-month re-application:** apply again when the window opens IF the debrief's gap is closed — companies track prior candidates, and a visibly-better second attempt is a strong signal. Reference the first loop honestly in the recruiter call: "I interviewed in March; the gap was X, and here's what I've built since." Recruiters remember candor.

## The rejection that isn't about you

Some rejections are headcount freezes, internal candidates, or the req closing — the recruiter going quiet mid-loop (the ghost, not the no). Those carry no information about your performance; log them as noise and spend zero debrief time. You can't fix a freeze; you CAN fix a fumbled deep-dive. Know which one you got.
